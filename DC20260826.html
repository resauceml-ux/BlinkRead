the seventh chapter introduces one of the most philosophically ambitious concepts in the entire book, what Tolle calls the unmanifested, and explores the various portals through which human consciousness can access this dimension of existence that lies beyond and beneath all the specific forms and contents of ordinary experience. the unmanifested is Tolles term for the formless, sourceless ground of being from which all manifest reality, all specific forms and phenomena, all thoughts, feelings, perceptions, and physical objects, continuously arises and into which it continuously returns. it is not a place or a state but rather the dimensionless depth of existence itself, the that which is aware prior to and independently of anything specific that awareness contains. Tolle acknowledges that the unmanifested cannot be adequately described through language, since language is itself a product of the manifested dimension and operates entirely within the realm of specific forms and distinct concepts. any description of the unmanifested is necessarily a description of something that points toward rather than captures its subject, a finger pointing at the moon rather than the moon itself. with this qualification clearly in place, Tolle proceeds to identify several specific portals through which human consciousness can make direct contact with this dimension, even if that contact cannot be fully articulated in conceptual terms after the fact. the first and most fundamental portal Tolle identifies is the present moment itself, the now that the entire book has been pointing toward. genuine presence, fully inhabited without the overlay of mental commentary and psychological time, is itself a contact with the unmanifested because the now, as Tolle has argued throughout, is not itself a moment in time but the timeless dimension within which all specific moments arise. the deeper one goes into genuine presence, the more apparent this timeless, formless quality becomes, and the more clearly the distinction between the manifested content of experience and the unmanifested awareness within which that content appears makes itself felt. the second portal Tolle describes is dreamless sleep, the state of deep, unconscious rest in which all specific mental content ceases and consciousness temporarily merges with the formless ground of being without any accompanying awareness of the merger. Tolle argues that this is why deep dreamless sleep is so profoundly restorative in a way that dreaming sleep, despite its vividness, often is not. in dreamless sleep, consciousness temporarily leaves the exhausting activity of manifested experience and rests in the unmanifested source from which it continuously draws its energy and aliveness. the refreshment people feel after genuinely deep sleep reflects this temporary merger with the source, a nourishment that no amount of pleasant dreaming can substitute for. a third portal that Tolle introduces in this chapter is the gap between thoughts, the brief moment of silence that occurs between one thought ending and the next beginning. for most people, this gap is so brief and so immediately filled with the next thought that it passes entirely unnoticed. but Tolle argues that the gap, however brief, is a genuine window into the unmanifested, a momentary cessation of the manifested activity of thought through which the formless dimension of awareness briefly shows through. meditation practices across many traditions have worked with this gap, using various techniques to lengthen and deepen it, essentially training the meditator to recognize and inhabit the silent awareness that is always present between thoughts and that the continuous noise of habitual thinking perpetually obscures. Tolle also discusses the relationship between the manifested world and the unmanifested in more extended philosophical terms, arguing that the manifest world is not separate from or opposed to its unmanifested source but is a continuous arising and expression of it, the way waves are expressions of the ocean rather than separate things that happen to exist on its surface. the world of form that ordinary consciousness takes as the whole of reality is, in this framework, only the surface of a much deeper reality that includes its own source, and genuine contact with that source through any of the portals Tolle describes transforms the relationship to the manifested world from one of anxious grasping and resisting to one of open, clear eyed participation in what he describes as the play of the universe. the chapter closes with the reminder that contact with the unmanifested is not a rare or exotic achievement reserved for advanced spiritual practitioners but something that every human being touches regularly, in the gap between thoughts, in the depths of dreamless sleep, in the moments of genuine stillness that occasionally arise even in the most busy and conventionally oriented life. what the practice of presence makes possible is simply the recognition and the deliberate cultivation of a contact that was always already occurring without being noticed. Tolle closes this discussion of the unmanifested with a reminder that these portals are not exotic achievements reserved for advanced practitioners but ordinary features of human experience that most people pass through daily without recognizing their significance. the invitation the chapter extends is simply to notice these moments when they occur, to recognize the gap between thoughts, the depth of dreamless sleep, and the stillness of genuine presence as genuine contacts with the source from which all experience continuously arises, rather than allowing them to pass entirely unnoticed as they typically do.
