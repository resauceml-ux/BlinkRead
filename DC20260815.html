the twelfth chapter addresses what Hill considers the most powerful and most mysterious of all the mental faculties that contribute to achievement, the subconscious mind, which he describes as the connecting link between the finite mind of the individual human being and the broader intelligence of the universe that he refers to throughout the book as infinite intelligence. this is a somewhat mystical framing that Hill maintains consistently and without apology throughout the chapter, arguing that the evidence for the reality of this connection comes not from metaphysical speculation but from the direct testimony of countless individuals who have successfully used the subconscious mind as a channel for receiving ideas, insights, and practical guidance that exceeded what their ordinary conscious reasoning could have produced. Hill opens with a description of the subconscious mind as a vast, continuously operating field that receives and records every thought, every emotion, and every experience that the conscious mind generates or encounters, filing these impressions according to their emotional intensity and making them available as inputs to the creative and directive processes through which practical action is guided. unlike the conscious mind, which is capable of critical evaluation and selective attention, the subconscious accepts and acts upon whatever material reaches it, whether positive or negative, constructive or destructive, true or false. this undiscriminating receptivity is what makes deliberate, intentional communication with the subconscious both so important and so powerful. the chapter introduces Hills classification of the seven major positive emotions, desire, faith, love, sex, enthusiasm, romance, and hope, as the primary tools through which the conscious mind communicates most effectively with the subconscious. Hill argues that these emotions are the language the subconscious most readily responds to, and that thoughts and instructions accompanied by genuine emotional charge of these varieties leave much deeper and more durable impressions than purely intellectual, emotionally neutral communications. this is why the autosuggestion technique described in an earlier chapter specifically requires genuine emotional engagement and not merely mechanical repetition. Hill also identifies seven major negative emotions, fear, jealousy, hatred, revenge, greed, superstition, and anger, as the primary contaminants that undermine the effectiveness of the subconscious as a creative and directive force. the subconscious does not evaluate the quality of the emotions it receives. it acts on whatever it is given with equal energy and creativity, which means that a mind dominated by negative emotions of these varieties will tend to generate plans, behaviors, and practical outcomes that reflect those emotional inputs rather than the positive outcomes the person may consciously desire. managing the emotional inputs that reach the subconscious is therefore not merely a matter of personal wellbeing but a practical necessity for anyone who wishes to use this faculty effectively in service of their goals. the chapter addresses the practical question of how to communicate most effectively with the subconscious in service of specific desired outcomes. Hills primary recommendation is the sustained practice of autosuggestion, the emotionally charged repetition of clearly stated desired outcomes that gradually and reliably impresses these outcomes on the subconscious mind until they are treated as established facts requiring practical expression rather than as distant possibilities requiring further evaluation. the emotional component of this practice is critical, since without it the communication does not reach the subconscious in a form it can act on most powerfully. the chapter closes by connecting the subconscious to the concept of creative intelligence in a broader sense, suggesting that the extraordinary creative insights that appear to arrive from nowhere in moments of inspired breakthrough, the sudden solutions to problems that conscious reasoning had failed to crack, the ideas that appear fully formed in the minds of artists and inventors and entrepreneurs who have been deeply engaged with a creative challenge, represent the subconscious mind delivering the results of its own vast, continuous processing to the surface of conscious awareness. cultivating the habit of deliberately programming this processing through emotionally charged, positively directed autosuggestion is, in Hills framework, one of the most important skills any person seeking significant achievement can develop.
