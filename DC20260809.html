the sixth chapter introduces imagination as what Hill calls the workshop of the mind, the faculty through which desire is translated into plans and plans into concrete action. Hill argues that imagination is more than a pleasant mental capacity for creative daydreaming. it is the instrument through which all human achievement, from the most practical business venture to the most extraordinary scientific discovery, is first conceived and then progressively developed into the definite form required for action. nothing significant ever existed in the physical world before it first existed as a clear, developed image in the imagination of the person who brought it into being. Hill distinguishes two forms of imagination that he considers fundamentally different in their nature and their function. the first is synthetic imagination, the faculty through which existing concepts, experiences, ideas, and plans are recombined in new arrangements. most of what is commonly called creative thinking actually operates at this level, finding new ways of connecting things that already exist rather than generating genuinely new elements from nothing. the second form is creative imagination, a capacity that Hill describes in more mystical terms, connecting it to the concept of infinite intelligence and suggesting that truly original ideas, those that seem to arrive fully formed from outside ones ordinary stream of thinking, come through this second faculty. for most practical purposes, Hill argues that synthetic imagination is the primary tool available and the one most relevant to financial achievement. the greatest fortunes, he contends, have almost invariably been built not on entirely unprecedented inventions but on new combinations and new applications of existing ideas, products, and methods. the person who can look at an existing situation and see a combination or application that others have missed, who can identify a need that existing products or services do not adequately address and then assemble the known elements into a new configuration that meets that need, is exercising synthetic imagination in its most practically valuable form. the chapter introduces the principle that ideas are the beginning of all fortunes and that the capacity to generate and develop ideas is therefore among the most economically valuable capacities a person can cultivate. Hill supports this with examples from american business history, describing how individuals who brought specific ideas to the marketplace at the right moment, often ideas that seemed simple or obvious in retrospect, were able to build enormous wealth from what started as nothing more than a clearly articulated mental image of a possible combination or application that nobody had yet pursued. Hill also addresses the relationship between imagination and the other principles he has introduced. imagination does not operate in isolation. it is most productive when directed by a clear, burning desire, energized by faith, and organized by the kind of definite planning that will be addressed in subsequent chapters. unfocused imagination, the kind that drifts pleasantly among interesting possibilities without the discipline of definiteness or the motivation of genuine desire, tends to produce nothing of practical value. focused imagination, applied consistently to a specific, definite goal with strong emotional investment, is what Hill means when he describes this faculty as the workshop in which all plans are created and all achievements are first born. the chapter closes by encouraging readers to exercise imagination deliberately and regularly in the service of their specific goals, spending time each day in focused mental exploration of the various ways in which their desired outcome might be achieved, the various combinations of knowledge, relationships, and resources that might serve their purpose, and the specific next steps that current imagination suggests. Hill argues that this deliberate imaginative practice is itself one of the most productive activities available to anyone pursuing significant achievement, since the plans and combinations it generates are often qualitatively superior to anything that purely analytical thinking produces working from the same information.
