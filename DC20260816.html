the thirteenth chapter is among the most speculative and most philosophically ambitious in the entire book, presenting Hills theory of the human brain as a broadcasting and receiving station for thought vibrations, a concept he grounds in the analogy of radio technology, which was still a relatively new and somewhat mysterious phenomenon when the book was written. Hill draws a parallel between the way radio transmitters broadcast signals that can be picked up by appropriately tuned receivers anywhere within range, and what he presents as the capacity of the human brain to both broadcast and receive thought vibrations of various frequencies and intensities. the chapter begins by describing what Hill considers the two primary functions of the brain in relation to the achievement principles he has described throughout the book. the first is the broadcasting function, through which the brain, stimulated to a sufficiently high level of emotional intensity by the forces of desire, faith, and sexual energy, sends out thought vibrations that can be picked up by other brains and can also, Hill argues, reach what he calls infinite intelligence, the broader field of creative intelligence from which the most inspired and most original ideas come to those who have trained themselves to receive them. the second function is the receiving function, through which the brain in its most highly stimulated states becomes capable of picking up thought vibrations from other stimulated minds, a phenomenon that Hill presents as the deeper explanation for the creative power of the mastermind alliance. when two or more minds are brought into harmonious, highly motivated coordination around a shared definite purpose, Hill argues, the combined broadcasting and receiving activity of their stimulated brains creates a field of heightened creative receptivity that makes each member more capable of generating and receiving inspired ideas than they would be working alone. Hill acknowledges throughout the chapter that the scientific basis for some of his claims about brain broadcasting and telepathic reception was not fully established at the time of writing, though he points to research in the field of brain science and to anecdotal evidence from prominent scientists and inventors as suggesting that the phenomenon he describes is real. he notes that Thomas Edison himself believed in the reality of thought transmission between minds and had expressed this view in various contexts, which Hill presents as authoritative endorsement from one of the most technically sophisticated and empirically minded individuals he had studied. the chapter also introduces the concept of the solar plexus as a secondary broadcasting station for thought vibrations, particularly emotional ones, a concept that reflects the folk understanding of the solar plexus as a center of gut feeling and emotional intuition that was common in Hills era. he argues that this secondary broadcasting function explains certain experiences of emotional transmission between people that ordinary communication cannot account for, such as the immediate sense of a persons character or intention that sometimes precedes any direct verbal interaction. Hills primary practical recommendation from this chapter is that the quality and the intensity of the thought vibrations one broadcasts, and therefore the quality of what one receives in return, are directly determined by the level of emotional stimulation of the broadcasting brain. this means that the practices recommended throughout the book for elevating the emotional tone of the mind, the cultivation of burning desire, the development of genuine faith, the conscious harnessing of sexual and romantic energy, serve a dual purpose. they not only motivate and energize action toward specific goals. they also tune the brain to the highest possible frequency of creative receptivity, making it most capable of receiving the inspired ideas and intuitive guidance that, in Hills framework, represent the highest and most valuable form of intelligence available to human beings.
