the final section of the power of now draws together the threads that have run through all the preceding chapters into a concluding reflection on what sustained practice of acceptance and surrender actually produces over the arc of a life genuinely committed to these principles. Tolle is careful not to promise a destination of permanent, unbroken peace and happiness, since such a promise would both misrepresent the nature of the practice and set up an expectation that the inevitable difficulties of continued human life would inevitably disappoint. what he does promise, based on his own experience and his observation of others who have applied these teachings consistently, is a gradual, deepening transformation in the quality of inner experience that makes even the most difficult circumstances more navigable and the most ordinary circumstances richer and more fully alive. the chapter addresses the question that naturally arises for anyone who has followed the books argument this far with genuine interest, how does one actually sustain this practice across the full range of daily life, through the demanding and the mundane, the joyful and the difficult, the special and the completely ordinary. Tolle acknowledges that this is the real challenge and the real work, not the relatively easy moments of presence that occur during meditation or in situations of unusual beauty or intensity, but the maintenance of present awareness during the routine, repetitive, unremarkable moments that constitute the majority of any actual human life. his primary observation about this sustaining practice is that it is not so much a matter of trying harder but of returning more readily. the mind will drift. thoughts will pull attention away from the present moment into past and future countless times in any given day. the practice is not to prevent this from happening, since resistance to the mind drift is itself a form of resistance to what is, but to return more and more quickly and more and more effortlessly to the present moment each time the drift is noticed. each returning is itself a moment of genuine presence, however brief, and over time the habit of returning becomes more natural and the drift becomes less dominant. Tolle also returns in this final section to the relationship between individual awakening and the broader condition of human consciousness, a theme he has touched on throughout the book but not developed at length. he argues that each person who genuinely shifts their center of gravity from ego based identification with thought to present moment awareness contributes something to the collective field of human consciousness that extends beyond their individual life. this is not a claim about supernatural influence but about the very practical reality that people who inhabit genuine presence affect the quality of their interactions with everyone around them, that presence is genuinely contagious and that its effects ripple outward in ways that are real even when they cannot be precisely tracked or measured. the closing pages offer what might be considered the books final practical instruction, not a technique or a method but a simple pointing toward what has always been available and what no amount of technique can substitute for. the now is here. it has always been here. it will always be here, since the now is not a point in time that passes but the timeless dimension within which all points in time arise and dissolve. the thinking mind moves through time, accumulating past and anticipating future, and there is a kind of richness and complexity in that temporal movement that is genuinely part of what it means to be human. but beneath and prior to that movement, available in every moment of genuine turning toward it, is the stillness and the aliveness and the peace of the present moment itself. this is the teaching the book has been pointing toward from its first page. not a belief to be adopted, not a technique to be mastered, not an achievement to be reached at some future point when sufficient spiritual progress has been made. simply this moment, as it actually is, met with full, open, undefended presence. the simplicity of this teaching is both its most striking quality and its greatest challenge, since the thinking mind, oriented toward complexity, achievement, and becoming, tends to look through and past something this immediate and this ordinary. but the people who have genuinely stopped looking past it, who have learned to be here rather than perpetually on the way to somewhere else, consistently report that what they found in the stopping was not what they expected and was more than they had hoped for. the power of now closes not with a summary of its teachings but with a final pointing, a gesture toward the reality that all the words have been circling. that reality is not a concept or a state to be achieved in the future. it is the simple, always available presence of this moment, exactly as it is, met with full, open, undefended awareness. that is all the book has ever been pointing toward. and it is, as Tolle discovered on that night of transformation in his late twenties, more than enough.
