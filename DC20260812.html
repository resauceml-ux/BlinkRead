the ninth chapter addresses what Hill considers the single most important quality separating those who eventually achieve significant success from the much larger number who possess comparable talent, knowledge, and opportunity but fail to translate these advantages into meaningful results. that quality is persistence, the sustained, determined continuation of effort in the face of opposition, temporary failure, and the inevitable discouragement that any genuinely ambitious undertaking eventually produces. Hill does not present persistence as an admirable but essentially secondary quality that supplements more fundamental capabilities. he presents it as a primary force without which all the other principles he has described remain ultimately insufficient. the chapter opens with a stark observation drawn from Hills research, that the majority of people who fail to achieve their goals do not fail because their goals were wrong, their plans were flawed, or their circumstances were insurmountable. they fail because they stop. they encounter the first serious obstacle, the first significant failure, the first wave of discouragement and doubt, and they stop, often rationalizing the decision to stop as wisdom or realism rather than recognizing it for what it actually is, the surrender of a goal that had not yet been pursued with sufficient persistence to reach the point where results become visible. Hill identifies the basis of persistence as the combination of the power of will and the quality of desire. will without desire produces mechanical, joyless effort that eventually exhausts itself. desire without will produces initial enthusiasm that dissolves at the first serious obstacle. the combination of the two, a burning desire for a specific, definitively conceived goal backed by the disciplined will to continue pursuing it through whatever opposition presents itself, produces the quality of persistence that Hill describes as virtually unstoppable in its long term effect. the chapter provides four specific steps for developing persistence that Hill distills from his research. the first is a definite purpose backed by burning desire. the second is a definite plan expressed in continuous action. the third is a mind closed tightly against all negative and discouraging influences, including the well meaning but limiting opinions of family, friends, and others who have not achieved what one is pursuing. the fourth is a friendly alliance with at least one person who will encourage one to follow through on the plan and purpose. Hill also introduces in this chapter the concept of what he calls the power of persistence specifically as it relates to faith, arguing that these two qualities have a reciprocal, reinforcing relationship that is crucial to understand. persistence develops faith because every act of continuing despite difficulty, every choice to try again after a setback, every refusal to accept a temporary defeat as a permanent conclusion, strengthens the implicit belief that the goal is achievable and that ones own efforts are adequate to achieve it. and faith, once strengthened through this process, in turn makes persistence easier, since it becomes progressively less effortful to continue when the inner conviction that the effort will eventually be rewarded is stronger and more deeply rooted. the chapter closes with a reflection on what Hill describes as the tests that nature appears to apply to people who are pursuing significant goals, suggesting that opposition and difficulty are not merely unfortunate obstacles but in some sense necessary filters that ensure that only those with sufficient genuine desire and sufficient genuine persistence reach the goals worth reaching. the person who gives up at the first sign of serious opposition reveals by that giving up that their desire was not genuinely strong enough to deserve the outcome they were pursuing. the person who persists through opposition of any magnitude eventually earns a quality of faith in themselves and their capabilities that no amount of theoretical preparation could have produced.
